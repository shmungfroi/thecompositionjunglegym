## Method (my own)

* **Broad sweep** (`sweep.py`, the earlier checker's sweep run again on this build). Every room at Stages 4, 5 and 6, the Keys tabs, climbs 1–13,
  10 forms, Analysis (map, prog and 10 scores) and the Ear Lab (7 drills × 3 rungs, answered). It took 2,447 snapshots of visible prose,
  buttons and titles, headings and svg text. `analyse_old.py` holds the term regexes (a US term alone, first mention, headings,
  short forms, abbreviations).
* **Targeted terms** (`terms2.py`): 480 Ear Lab result panels (Stages 4–6, Cadences and Blind analysis, rungs 1–3; Correct, Not this
  time, Shown and Nearly states, every answer button in turn). It also reads the rhythm tile hovers, the chord-quality and rhythm
  feedback, the rondo room and the Analysis cadence tables. Then it checks all 24 circle keys against music21 10.5 and the key line at 1000 px.
* **Lint probe** (`lintprobe.py`): content_lint's own `US_ALONE` / `us_allowed` / `US_SHORT` run on 56 strings I wrote, plus 10
  short-form strings.
* **F** (`cp13.py`, `cp13_an.py`): every climb 2 ladder hover in 60 states (each mode × above/below × species 1–5), 4,480 distinct titles.
  It fuzzes `c13Pair` with 800 random two-voice lines (random accidentals, compounds, holds; 7,568 issues) and runs `c13CanonCheck` over 4 keys,
  6 modes, 8 intervals and 3 delays (10,669 issues). It also runs 13 countersubject cases I built by hand (`cscases.json`): P5 above, A4/d5, P4 above,
  P11 above, a CS that crosses below, P12, a minor-key A2, d7, augmented octave and unison, Dorian, a weak-beat 5th, and E♭ major P11.
  Every named interval was compared with music21 (`Interval(lower, upper).name`, turned into words).
* **Decision 12** (`fn12.py`, `fn12b.py`, `anharm.py`, `c78.py`): `analyse()` and `homes()` run on 43 numerals in all 24 keys. The script
  visits the Seed (9 chords), Keys scales (17), Gym (3 progressions), Oracle (full decks, major and minor), the Voicing lab, climbs 3, 5, 6, 7,
  8 and 11 (all versions, plus `c11Draw` with ♭VII and v), Analysis (10 scores with the harmony layer, prog) and the Ear Lab
  generators (400 items × cad/bass × 3 rungs). In each view it collects every visible element whose computed color, fill, stroke,
  border-top or background is `#bd6b97`, `#9e4776` or the old `#7fb3c8`, and works out its contrast against the first opaque ancestor background.
* Reference: content_lint, counterpoint_test, unit_test, and terms_test with `TERMS_FAST=1` all pass on the copy (0 failures). My verdicts do not rest on them.

## Findings

### Must

**M-12. The Seed calls a minor-key Vsus4 or V5 "the minor v", and colours it differently from the same chord in major.**
`isMD()` gives MD to any root on the 5th with no leading note, and the Seed then shows the minor-v text for it. Rendered (C minor):
"In C minor, Gsus4 is Vsus4, a modal dominant, a soft pull: the minor v, built on the 5th of the natural minor…" and "In C minor, G5
is V5, … the minor v…". Gsus4 and G5 are not minor chords, and the numeral on screen is upper-case. In the same chip row, C major's
Vsus4 / V5 (also without a leading note) is red D. A suspended V is a dominant (its 4th is a suspension that resolves to the 3rd), in
either mode. Fix: at deg 4, give MD only when the chord holds the minor 3rd above the root (`pcs.includes(mod(k+10,12))`), so v and v7
qualify and sus/5 chords stay D as in major. Or keep MD for them but use the general MD text, not MDv. (`index.html` l.2018 `isMD`,
l.2038 text choice.) VIIsus4 / VII5 → MD with the "subtonic VII" text is acceptable.

### Should

**S-T1. The `syn` rhythm tile hover drops a value.** "quaver, crotchet, quaver · US: eighth note, quarter note" should read "…· US:
eighth note, quarter note, eighth note". The pass writes one US form per distinct term. The other compound tiles are right only because
their terms do not repeat. Fix in the `data-tt` path: map each listed value in order, or give the tile a hand-written title.

**S-T2. The content lint has gaps and false positives** (my probe, `/home/claude/checks/r2/lintprobe.py`).
Misses: "an 8th note", "a 16th note", "two sixteenths", "quarter&nbsp;note" / U+00A0, "the downbeat of measure one", "the first
measure of the song", "a measure of rest". The short-form check misses "(US:half)", "(US: Half)" and "(US: deceptive.)".
False positives: "In the US this is called a half cadence." (labelled, but not straight after "US"), "the whole note-for-note copy",
"an authentic Baroque sound", "it measures 4 cm". None of these occurs in the app now, so the lint is sound for today's text. It is not
yet the "every term, no false positives" net the spec asks for. Fix: add `\b(?:8|16)th[ -]notes?`, `\b(?:eighth|sixteenth)s\b`,
`[\s ]|&nbsp;` as the separator, and "measure (one…twelve)" / "(first|last|…) measure of the (song|piece|phrase)". Make
`US_SHORT` case-insensitive with `\s*` after the colon. Allow "in the US … called".

**S-F1. The countersubject note "This is a 5th that became a 4th when the voices swapped" is wrong in two real cases.**
(a) The CS is written an 11th above the subject (C4/F5): the inverted position is C4 against F4, with the CS **still above**. It gets IC1 and
the note, but nothing was a 5th. The issue is already reported above as "a perfect 11th". (b) The CS is written **below** the subject
(G3 under C4): the first list says "CS above: … G3 against C4 is a perfect 4th" (the CS is below), and the inverted list says "C4 against
G2 is a perfect 11th … This is a 5th that became a 4th" (it was a 4th that became an 11th). Fix: give IC1 and the note only when the
matching cell in the "above" list was a perfect 5th or 12th (or test `iv.g7===4` and the CS below the subject in the inverted pair).
Name the position by actual register, not by list.

**S-F2. The same note is added to weak-beat I2 issues.** "CS below (inverted): Bar 1, beat 2: both voices move into a dissonance (a
perfect 4th)… This is a 5th that became a 4th…: avoid 5ths on strong beats in invertible counterpoint." The advice is about strong
beats, but this issue is on beat 2 and is about both voices moving. The old regex did the same, so this predates the change. Fix: add the
note to IC1 (strong-beat) issues only.

### Could

* **C-F1.** `C2QN.x = 'doubly altered'` gives "a doubly altered 4th" where music21 says "doubly augmented" (AA4) or "doubly diminished"
  (dd5). It cannot happen with climb 13's own lines (0 in canon and ladder runs), only with free chromatic input. Splitting `x` into
  AA/dd would make it exact.
* **C-F2.** Ladder hovers still reach "perfect 18th", "major 16th", "major 17th" (earlier C4). They are correct, but "a perfect 4th plus two octaves" reads better.
* **C-12a.** `earNumFn`: map v/v7/VII/VII7 to MD and fix ♭VII → T before any Ear Lab item uses them.
* **C-T1.** The climb 3 "In the US" rows are still there (earlier C1).

### X-12. Major-key MD (builder extension, reported, not counted as a failure)

`analyse()` gives MD to the borrowed **v, v7, ♭VII and ♭VII7 in every major key** (12/12 keys, `borrowed:true`). Where a student sees it:
* **Gym**: the palette's ♭VII (level ≥ 5), the cards (rose top, "soft pull · modal dominant") and the frame numerals (seen with I–♭VII–IV–I–v–I in C).
* **Oracle**: the ♭VII card in C major shows suit "Soft pull" and hint "no leading note: a softer pull home, a modal sound". It still counts as the colour card for drawing (`isCol`).
* **Climb 11**: numerals for ♭VII and ♭VII7 (in the level 5/6 vocabulary) and v.
* **Voicing lab** (SATB numerals) and Analysis "My progression" function row, for any major progression with these chords. Builder also lists climbs 4 and 5.

Theory: this matches the MD definition (dominant side of the key, no leading note). The Mixolydian/Aeolian v and ♭VII are the standard
"modal dominants" of rock and folk; ♭VII7 is the jazz backdoor dominant, a soft pull too. Inconsistencies Justin should weigh:
1. **Climb 8**, the climb that teaches ♭VII ("The rock ♭VII"), draws the ♭VII bar **violet** (colour), because `c8Bars` forces C for any chord with a
   new note. Its own table describes v as "a soft, modal dominant with no leading note". So the same chord is rose in the Gym and in climb
   11, and violet in climb 8. Climb 7 uses the same rule.
2. The Analysis numeral key says "the minor v and the subtonic VII **of a minor key**", and the Seed legend says "(minor v, VII…)". A rose ♭VII in a major
   progression has no matching key text.
3. Borrowed iv, ♭VI and ♭III in major stay violet (colour), while borrowed v and ♭VII get a function colour. That is defensible (MD is a
   dominant-side family), but it is the one place where a borrowed chord gets a function colour.

If Justin keeps MD minor-only, it is the `k.mode==='maj'&&…isMD` clause in `analyse()` (l.2391).
