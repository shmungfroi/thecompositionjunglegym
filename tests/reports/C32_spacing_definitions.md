# C32 - How do authoritative sources define "close" and "open" spacing? (boundary case: S-T exactly an octave)

Researched 21 Nov 2026 for the Composition Jungle Gym (NSW Stages 4-6). Terminology: Australian first, US in brackets (close/open spacing = US close/open position or structure).

## Honesty note on sourcing

- I could not open any of the named print textbooks (Kostka & Payne, Aldwell & Schachter, Laitz, Clendinning & Marvin). None of them is freely readable online.
- Kostka & Payne is represented only by a McGraw-Hill companion-site glossary and a student study packet that repeats it. The glossary page itself names no book. - I found no verified text for Aldwell & Schachter, Laitz or Clendinning & Marvin, so they are not in the table. Do not cite them on the strength of this report. Check the books themselves (or the Laitz/A&S instructor materials) before the app claims any of them.

- Two fetches (Baur and the Sweethaven page) reported the boundary behaviour in the tool's words, not as a clean quote. Those rows are flagged.
- No NSW, NESA, ABRSM, Trinity or UK A-level document I could reach defines close/open spacing. Details are below.

## 2. Plain-language summary for a learning teacher

- There are two different ideas hiding under "spacing", and the sources mix them up.
  1. **Spacing limits (an error rule):** S-A and A-T must each be an octave or less, and T-B may reach a twelfth. Open Music Theory, Puget Sound, Minnesota State, Iowa State and UQ teach only this. They never name close and open. A gap of exactly one octave between adjacent voices is fine in every source that says so.
  2. **Close vs open (a label):** a description of one chord's shape. The label is about the S-T span, with the bass left out, in most sources.
- **The label has no single definition.** Three kinds exist.
  - *Octave rule, cut at "less than an octave" (Kostka-style).* Close means S-T is smaller than an octave. Open means an octave or more. An exact octave is **open**.
  - *Octave rule, cut at "an octave or less" (RWU, Sweethaven and similar).* An exact octave is **close**.
  - *"Can a chord tone be inserted?" rule (SFCM, LIU).* Close means every chord tone is already packed in. Open means there is a gap you could fill.
- **The exact-octave case really does split the sources.** The two best-documented definitions contradict each other: the Kostka-style glossary says open, RWU says close. Baur's wording (as summarised) puts the octave in both categories. This is not a gap in the research. It is a genuine disagreement in the literature.
- **The insertion test settles it in favour of "open".** If S and T are the same pitch class an octave apart, the one chord tone not yet used must fit somewhere inside that octave, so there is always room to insert it. Under the insertion test an exact octave is therefore open (section 3 shows the argument). This is my deduction, not a quote.
- **A three-way scheme exists, but only loosely.** *Harmony and Musicianship with Solfege* adds "semi-open" (and a "mixed spacing" chapter) for chords where the upper voices double the octave. No source I found makes a formal close / open / neither classification by distance.
- **Nothing NSW-specific defines it.** The NSW syllabus support and the AMEB syllabus I could read use the terms "open or closed position" or "spacing" without defining them. Students will therefore meet whatever definition their textbook or teacher uses.

## 3. Why exact-octave S-T = open under the insertion test

Take a triad with S and T on the same pitch class an octave apart, for example S = E5, T = E4. The alto is then either the root or the fifth. The remaining chord tone has to lie somewhere within the E4 to E5 octave, because an octave contains every pitch class. It therefore falls between two adjacent upper voices, and a chord element could be inserted. That is open by the SFCM test. This is my own reasoning, not a quote from SFCM. It holds for triads. For seventh chords an exact S-T octave means doubling and an omitted tone, and the same argument goes through for the omitted tone.

## 4. Recommendation for the app

**Keep the current rule: S-T < octave = close; S-T >= octave = open.** Reasons:

1. **It matches the most explicit, widely used definition I could find** (the Kostka-style glossary: "less than an octave" is close, "an octave or more" is open). That book is the standard North American reference and its terminology is the one most NSW teachers will have met in tertiary study.
2. **It agrees with the insertion-test definitions** (SFCM, LIU "skipped tone"), so the app's rule and the "could I fit another chord tone in?" test give the same answer. The RWU "octave or less" rule does not.
3. **It is unambiguous and testable.** A strict less-than boundary gives a single clean classification, with no overlap. Baur-style wording ("octave or less" and "octave or more") produces a double classification.
4. **It cleanly separates the label from the error rule.** An exact S-T octave still satisfies the S-A and A-T limits automatically, so nothing is "wrong" about it. The app should not flag it as an error.
5. **No NSW source overrides it.** NESA documents and AMEB leave the terms undefined, so no local authority contradicts the choice.

Caveats and suggested app handling:

- **Name the convention in the UI** (a one-line tooltip): "In this app, S-T of exactly one octave counts as open (as in Kostka's definition). Some texts call this close."
- **Do not mark either classification wrong in student work or checking.** Source disagreement means a student taught the "octave or less" rule is not mistaken. Treat the label as informational, and reserve error flags for the S-A / A-T > octave and T-B > twelfth rules.
- **Keep the label separate from the "close score / open score" staff-layout terms.** UK and AMEB-trained students may use those terms for something else.
- **Consider an optional teaching extra** rather than changing the core rule: show a "no gap to fill" insertion-test indicator, and/or a "semi-open" tag for I6/IV6/V6/vi doublings, borrowing the Solfege book's idea. Do not present "semi-open" as a standard term.
- **Before final wording, verify the Kostka attribution in the physical or licensed text** (the glossary does not name its book) and read Aldwell & Schachter, Laitz and Clendinning & Marvin directly.

## Five-line summary

1. Sources split on the exact-octave S-T case: the Kostka-style glossary says open ("an octave or more"), RWU and others say close ("an octave or less"), and Baur's wording overlaps.
2. The "no chord tone can be inserted" test (SFCM, LIU) also classes an exact octave as open, by my deduction; Open Music Theory, Puget Sound, Minnesota, Iowa and UQ avoid the labels and give only the adjacent-pair octave limit.
3. No NSW, NESA or AMEB document I could read defines close/open (AMEB uses "open or closed position" undefined); A&S, Laitz, Clendinning & Marvin, ABRSM, Trinity and A-level sources were not verified.
4. Only loose three-way schemes exist ("semi-open" and "mixed spacing" in Harmony and Musicianship with Solfege); no formal close/open/neither distance scheme found.
5. Recommendation: keep S-T < octave = close, S-T >= octave = open, label the convention in the UI, and never flag either classification as a student error.
