Phrygian cadence

You heard it stop on V, and that is right: this is an imperfect cadence (US: half). It is the special kind. In a minor key the bass steps down a semitone, fa to mi (iv6 to V), and that is called a Phrygian cadence.

Listening for it

  1. Start with the last chord. Home (I), the dominant (V), or somewhere else (vi)?
  2. Then the chord before it. V before I is perfect; IV before I is plagal.
  3. Follow the bass. Sing the last two bass notes. Fa falling a semitone to mi in a minor key is the Phrygian cadence.
  4. Listen for a delay. A cadential 6/4 holds the dominant bass under I, then V, before the close.

Australian names are used here. US names: perfect = authentic, imperfect = half, interrupted = deceptive.
